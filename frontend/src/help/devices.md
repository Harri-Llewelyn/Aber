## What this page is for

Devices are the machines, or parts of machines, that gateways send data for. Most are not created here. A device appears when a gateway announces it, and this page is where you turn that arrival into a proper record: named, placed, given a schema, and documented. In ISA-95 terms a device is a work unit, the lowest level, inside its cell.

The page is one card with two tabs. **Registered** lists the devices admitted to Aber, and opens on **Active**. **Quarantine** holds announced devices still waiting to be let in. While any are waiting, its tab turns the warning colour and shows a warning icon and the number waiting, so the list never jumps when a device arrives. The flag on Devices in the sidebar, and the search's **Quarantine** entry, open that tab. The card scrolls; the page does not.

## What the controls do

- **New Device** registers a device before it is installed. It needs permission to manage devices; without it, the button names the roles that have it. A device you register waits as **Awaiting first birth** until it publishes.
- **Click a row**, or move to it and press Enter or Space, to open the device's details panel. Its first action is **Edit Details** (or **Propose a Change**, for a role that may only propose), then **View Telemetry**, **Configuration Parameters**, **Digital Nameplate**, **Export…**, **Attached Links**, **View Audit Trail** and **Archive Device**. An archived device offers **Restore Device** first instead.
- **Location**, in the details panel, is one line: the area and cell the device belongs to, as Area › Cell. It says **(from gateway)** when the cell comes from the device's gateway rather than from the device itself. A device with no single cell reads Area-Wide or Site-Wide, a replay lane or a simulated device reads its lane, and one that nothing places reads **Unassigned**.
- **Serving Gateway** is the gateway that carries the device's data. **Location** is where the device is, which is a separate question. A location is one of three: in a cell (its own, or its gateway's); Area-Wide, serving a whole area; or Site-Wide, serving the whole site. Only one can be chosen, and a dropdown appears only where there is more than one answer, such as which cell or which area.
- **Schema** says what kind of device this is, and is what its metrics are checked against. Until it has one, its metrics are just names.
- **Schema Conformance** is set when editing a device. **Audit** records anything that breaks the schema and keeps the reading. **Enforce** records it and drops the reading that broke it: that reading is never stored and cannot be recovered. Only the offending metric is dropped; the rest of the message is stored. Enforce does nothing on a device with no schema.
- **Device Name** is yours to set. **Reported Name** and **Published ID** are what a quarantined device said about itself. They are kept separate on purpose: a name a device gives itself is a claim, not proof, and letting it replace yours would lose the difference.
- **Description** holds what no other field can, such as "spindle rebuilt 2026-03; runs warmer than its twin". Nothing reads it. For a manufacturer, serial number or firmware version, use **Digital Nameplate**.
- **The filters** all narrow the same list. The search box takes a name, a UUID or a Sparkplug ID. Beside it are status and **Needs attention**, which shows devices past their first-birth window, sending metrics their schema does not include, or with a problem in their location. The rest are under **Filters**: lifecycle (All, Active, Archived), type, schema, gateway, cell, and **Show replay lanes** once a playback has created any. The button says how many of those are set, and its **Clear** resets just them. Arriving from a schema's device count on the Schemas page sets the schema filter, and the line under the toolbar names the schema. **Clear filters** resets everything, lifecycle included.
- **View Telemetry** shows each metric's latest value, read once when it opens. Tick metrics and choose **Export CSV** to save them. **Resolution** chooses what is exported: raw readings, or one-minute, five-minute or hourly summaries. Each says how far back it actually has data, because raw readings are kept for much less time than the summaries: a range from last spring may exist only as hourly averages. If you choose a range that starts before the raw readings begin, the dialog says so, names the finest resolution that covers the whole range, and offers to switch. A summary export has its own columns (average, minimum, maximum and last for each period) and names the resolution in the file's first line, because the file is what gets passed on to someone else.
- **Export…** opens a dialog to choose the format. **AAS JSON (V3)** is the Asset Administration Shell as JSON. **AASX package** is the same shell packaged with any attached 3D model. **Bundle (with history)** is the AASX plus the device's Audit Trail, the readings still in the live database, and a manifest; a copy is kept beside the cold archive. The first two are open to every role. The bundle is disabled, with the reason, for a role that may not read the Audit Trail (the bundle contains it), and on a replay lane, which is a recording of a device rather than a device. The Archived Entities page's Export Bundle opens the same dialog.
- **The Quarantine tab** holds devices Aber does not know. A gateway announced a device that is not registered, so its readings were held rather than stored against a guess. **Approve & Onboard** admits it as a new device; if it is a device already registered under another name, the queue offers that match instead. **Reject** discards what was held. Nothing in the queue counts as plant history until it is approved. Both actions need permission; a disabled button names the roles that have it.

## What the states mean

- **Awaiting first birth**: the record exists, but the device has not published yet. Usually a device created before it was installed. It is marked as a warning once it is more than 24 hours old.
- **Publishing unmodelled metrics**: the device sends metric names its schema does not include. This is worked out when you look, not stored, so editing a schema updates its devices at once rather than at their next birth, which may be weeks away.
- **Quarantined**: Aber could not match an announcement to a registered device. Quarantine is about identity, not readings. The four reasons are an unknown device, a malformed identifier, an identity mismatch and a gateway mismatch. The reason is stored, not just logged, so it is still there tomorrow. Whether readings match the schema is **Schema Conformance**, above.
- **Archived**: out of service. It leaves the Active list, and the **Archived** filter option counts it. The Archived Entities page lists archived items of every kind.

**Sparkplug ID** is the address the device is reached at on the broker. It is made from the device's UUID and never changes, so renaming or moving a device breaks nothing that points at it. The **Sparkplug Topic Path** in the details panel is that ID joined to its gateway's group and edge node.
