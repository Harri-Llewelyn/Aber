## Summary

Devices are what the gateways publish. Most are not created here: they appear because something published a birth certificate underneath a gateway, and this page is where that arrival becomes a modelled asset -- named, located, classified against a schema, and documented. In ISA-95 terms a device is a work unit, the bottom of the hierarchy, inside the cell it is located in.

The **Quarantine queue** is the first card, above the **Devices** roster. It is always there, with a count, so the roster does not move when a device arrives. The roster opens on **Active** devices and scrolls inside its own card.

## What the controls do

- **New Device** registers a device ahead of its installation. It needs the permission to manage devices; without it the button names the roles that hold it. A device you register waits as **Awaiting first birth** until it publishes.
- **Click a row** to open the device's details panel. Its actions are **Edit Details** (or **Propose a Change**, for a role that may only propose), **View Telemetry**, **Configuration Parameters**, **Digital Nameplate**, the AAS exports, **Attached Links**, **View Audit Trail** and **Archive Device**. An archived device offers **Restore Device** instead.
- **Serving Gateway** is the gateway that carries the device's data. **Location** is where the device is, which is a separate question. Location is one of three: in a cell, either its own or the one inherited from the gateway; Area-Wide, serving a whole area; or Site-Wide, serving the whole campus. Only one can be chosen, and a dropdown appears only where the choice leaves more than one answer, such as which cell or which area.
- **Schema** classifies the device and is the contract its metrics are judged against. Until it has one, its metrics are names on a wire.
- **Schema Conformance** is set when editing a device. **Audit** records a violation of the schema and keeps the reading. **Enforce** records it and drops the offending reading: it is never written to the historian and cannot be recovered. Enforce does nothing on a device with no schema. Only the offending metric is dropped; the rest of the message is written.
- **Device Name** is yours to set. **Reported Name** and **Published ID** are what a quarantined device says about itself, and they are kept separate deliberately -- a self-declared name is a claim, not evidence, and letting it overwrite yours would lose the distinction.
- **Description** holds what no column can: "spindle rebuilt 2026-03; runs warmer than its twin". Nothing reads it. For a manufacturer, serial number or firmware version, use **Digital Nameplate**.
- **The filters** -- lifecycle (All, Active, Archived), status, type, schema, gateway and cell -- all narrow the same list, and the search box takes a name, a UUID or a Sparkplug ID. **Needs attention** shows devices past their first-birth window, matched by legacy name, publishing unmodelled metrics, or with a location finding. **Clear filters** resets them all, lifecycle included.
- **View Telemetry** opens each metric's latest value, read once when it opens. Tick metrics and use **Export CSV** to write them out. **Resolution** chooses what it exports: raw samples, or one-minute, five-minute or hourly buckets. Each is labelled with how far back it actually holds data, which is not the same for all four -- raw readings are kept for a fraction of the time the buckets are, so a range from last spring may exist only as hourly averages. Choose a range starting before the raw readings begin and the dialog says so, names the finest resolution that covers the whole range, and offers to switch. A bucket export carries its own columns (average, minimum, maximum and last per bucket) and names the resolution in the first line of the file, because the file is what gets forwarded to somebody else.
- **Export Bundle (with history)** downloads the device's AASX with its Audit Trail, the readings still in the live historian and a manifest, and keeps a copy beside the cold tier. It is offered only to a role that may read the Audit Trail, because the bundle carries it; **Export AAS JSON (V3)** and **Export AASX package** are open to every role.
- **The Quarantine queue** holds devices the platform does not know. A gateway published a birth for a device that is not registered, so the reading was held rather than recorded against a guess. **Approve & Onboard** admits it as a new device; where it is one already registered under another name, the queue offers that match to accept instead. **Reject** discards the payload. Nothing in the queue counts as plant history until it is approved. Both actions need permission; a disabled button names the roles that hold it.

## What the states mean

- **Awaiting first birth** -- the record exists and nothing has published. Usually a device created ahead of its installation. It is marked as a warning once it is more than 24 hours old.
- **Publishing unmodelled metrics** -- the device is sending metric names its assigned schema does not account for. This is derived when you look rather than stored, so editing a schema reclassifies its devices immediately instead of at their next birth, which may be weeks away.
- **Quarantined** -- the platform could not tie a birth to a registered device. Quarantine is about identity, not about the readings: the four reasons are an unknown device, a malformed identifier, an identity mismatch and a gateway mismatch. The reason is kept as a row rather than a log line, so it is still there tomorrow. Whether readings match the schema is **Schema Conformance**, above.
- **Archived** -- out of commission. It leaves the Active list, and the **Archived** filter option counts it. The Archived Entities page lists archived rows of every kind.

**Sparkplug ID** is the address the device is reachable at on the broker. It is generated from the device's UUID and never changes, so renaming or relocating a device does not break anything that was pointed at it. The **Sparkplug Topic Path** in the details panel is that ID joined to the serving gateway's group and edge node.
