## What this page is for

Devices are the machines, or parts of machines, that gateways send data for. Most are not created here. A device appears when a gateway announces it, and this page is where you turn that arrival into a proper record: named, placed, given a schema, and documented. In ISA-95 terms a device is a work unit, the lowest level, inside its cell.

The page is one card with two tabs. **Registered** lists the devices admitted to Aber, and opens on **Active**. **Quarantine** holds announced devices still waiting to be let in. While any are waiting, its tab turns the warning colour and shows a warning icon and the number waiting, so the list never jumps when a device arrives. The card scrolls; the page does not.

The sidebar flags Devices in two ways, and never adds the two numbers together. A red number counts the devices with a firing alert that nobody has acknowledged. It counts devices, not alerts, so a device with three alerts counts once. An alert someone has acknowledged, or that a suppression window keeps quiet, is not counted. The red number is for people who can acknowledge an alert: an Administrator, a Shopfloor Manager or an Operator. An amber number counts the devices waiting in quarantine, for people who can approve or reject them. With the sidebar's tabs listed, the red number sits on **Registered** and the amber one on **Quarantine**. With them closed, Devices shows the red number, and its tooltip names both. Without an alert it shows the amber number. The flag opens **Quarantine** when quarantine is all it says, and the search's **Quarantine** entry opens that tab too.

## What the controls do

- **New Device** registers a device before it is installed. It needs permission to manage devices; without it, the button names the roles that have it. A device you register waits as **Awaiting first birth** until it publishes.
- **Click a row**, or move to it and press Enter or Space, to open the device's details panel. Its first action is **Edit Details** (or **Propose a Change**, for a role that may only propose), then **View in History**, **Configuration Parameters**, **Digital Nameplate**, **Export…**, **Attached Links**, **View in Audit Trail** and **Archive Device**. An archived device offers **Restore Device** first instead. It asks first, and any replay lane recorded from the device comes back with it.
- **A column's heading sorts the table** by that column, on both tabs. Click it again to reverse the order, and a third time to go back to the usual order. A status sorts the most serious first, and an empty cell always sorts last. The page's address keeps the order, so a link or a reload shows the table the same way. The **Configuration Parameters** dialog sorts the same way, until it closes.
- **The Registered table fits the window.** With less room, it hides **Metric groups** first, then the UUID and **Cell**. The details panel shows each of them. A cut UUID ends in an ellipsis. Point at it for the whole of it.
- **Location**, in the details panel, is one line: the area and cell the device belongs to, as Area › Cell. It says **(from gateway)** when the place comes from the device's gateway rather than from the device itself. A device with no single cell reads Area-Wide or Site-Wide, a replay lane or a simulated device reads its lane, and one that nothing places reads **Unassigned**.
- **Unified Namespace Topic**, in the details panel, is where Aber republishes the device's readings for other programs, such as a BI tool. Each metric is published at the topic, then `/` and the metric's name. Click the topic to copy it. A device that is left out says why, and what fixes it: for example, its cell is in no area. Aber publishes these topics only if the bridge was turned on at install. While it is off, the panel says so.
- **The topic is made from names**: the site, the area, the cell and the device. Renaming any of them moves the topic, and a program still on the old topic gets no new readings. While the bridge is on, **Edit Details** says when a rename moves it. The **Sparkplug ID** never moves, so it stays the device's identity.
- **Serving Gateway** is the gateway that carries the device's data. **Location** is where the device is, which is a separate question. A location is one of four: **Follow Gateway**, **In a cell**, **Area-Wide** (serving a whole area) or **Site-Wide** (serving the whole site). Only one can be chosen. A dropdown appears only where there is more than one answer, such as which cell or which area.
- **Follow Gateway** is the first choice, and a new device has it. The device takes whatever place its gateway has: the gateway's cell, its area (Area-Wide) or the whole site (Site-Wide). A line under the choice says where that puts the device now, such as "Area-Wide in Assembly, from AssemblyFlow". If the gateway's location changes, the device moves with it. If the gateway has no location, or the device has no gateway, the device is **Unassigned**, and the line says why. A device set to a cell, Area-Wide or Site-Wide keeps its own place, whatever its gateway does. **In a cell** needs a cell chosen, and Save stays off until there is one.
- **Derived Metrics**, in the details panel, lists each metric Aber computes that one of the device's schemas names, such as parts per hour. It shows the current value with its unit as a symbol, such as **12 count/h**, and the window it is over: **over the last hour** for a rate and a share of time, **at the last whole minute** for a sum. It is read when you open the panel. A device with no value yet says **No value yet**. The panel has no such line for a device whose schemas name none.
- **Metric groups** are the groups of the metrics a device's schemas model: the part of each metric name before its first `/`. A schema that models `Axes/X/POSITION` and `Controller/EXECUTION` gives **Axes** and **Controller**. The roster has a column for them, the details panel a row, and **Filters** a list. A device has its groups as soon as it has a schema, before it first publishes, and editing a schema changes them at once. In **Edit Details**, a line under the schemas lists the groups the ticked schemas model. It changes as you tick, and it is absent when none is ticked.
- **Schemas** say what kind of device this is, and are what its metrics are checked against. Tick every schema that applies; a device can have several. A metric counts as modelled if any of them includes it. Until a device has one, its metrics are just names. Each schema attached or removed is recorded in the Audit Trail, under **Device schemas**.
- **To find a schema** while registering or editing a device, type in the search above the list. It finds a name, a word of a description, or a metric, such as `Spindle/LOAD`. A schema found by a metric says which one. Each row shows the version, how many metrics the schema models, and its description. A version not in force also says **Draft** or **Archived**. The schemas a device already has come first. A search hides rows and unticks none. The number beside **Schemas** counts every tick.
- **Schema Conformance** is set when editing a device, by someone who can edit devices. It cannot be proposed. **Audit** records anything that breaks the schema and keeps the reading. **Enforce** records it and drops the reading that broke it: that reading is never stored and cannot be recovered. Only the offending metric is dropped; the rest of the message is stored. Enforce does nothing on a device with no schema. A reading breaks the schema when its type is wrong. It also breaks it when it is outside the metric's permitted values or range, once a schema version carrying them is published (see the Schemas page). Under either setting, a reading published under the name of a derived metric is dropped, because Aber computes that metric. The Audit Trail records it as a schema rejection. The device should publish the reading under another name.
- **Device Name** is yours to set. **Reported Name** and **Published ID** are what a quarantined device said about itself. They are kept separate on purpose: a name a device gives itself is a claim, not proof, and letting it replace yours would lose the difference.
- **Description** holds what no other field can, such as "spindle rebuilt 2026-03; runs warmer than its twin". It is shown here, and i3X serves it as the device's description. Nothing acts on it. For a manufacturer, serial number or firmware version, use **Digital Nameplate**.
- **The filters** all narrow the same list. The search box takes a name, a UUID, a Sparkplug ID or a Published ID. Beside it are **Any status** and **Needs attention**. **Any status** also lists the two findings, **Publishing unmodelled metrics** and **Out of vocabulary**. **Needs attention** shows devices past their first-birth window, with a finding, or with a problem in their location. The rest are under **Filters**: lifecycle (Active and Archived, opening on Active), **Metric groups**, schema, gateway, cell, and **Show replay lanes** once a playback has created any. The button says how many of those are set, and its **Clear** resets just them. **Clear filters** resets everything, lifecycle included. A link saved when the list was called **Type** still opens the matching **Metric groups** or status choice.
- **Inside Filters, each list opens and closes.** Select a list's name to open it. The list that was open closes, so one is open at a time. A closed list still says what is ticked in it, such as **Gateway · 2 chosen**, or the name of the one value ticked. A name too long for the heading is cut short. Point at the heading, or move to it, to see the whole name. When Filters opens, the first list with something ticked is open, or else the lifecycle list. Lifecycle at **Active** does not count, because the page starts there.
- **Each list filter takes several values.** Tick every value you want: a device is listed if it matches any of them. Ticking nothing, or every lifecycle, shows them all. The filters combine, so a device must pass each one that is set. The number beside a value is how many devices have it among those the other filters let through. A long list has a search box, and the cell list is grouped by area: tick an area's heading to tick all its cells. Arriving from a schema's device count on the Schemas page sets the schema filter, and the line under the toolbar names the schema.
- **View in History** opens the [History](history.md) page on this device, with a chart of each metric over the last 24 hours. It is where you see a device's readings: each chart shows its metric's latest reading, and **Export CSV**, for an Administrator or a Shopfloor Manager, saves the readings as a file. See [Exporting readings](history.md#exporting-readings).
- **View 3D Model** opens the device's 3D model in a view that fills the window. It sits after **View in History** in the details panel. Nothing of the model loads until you open the view, and closing it stops it. Drag to turn the model, scroll to zoom, and right-drag to move it. It holds still until you move it. **Reset view** puts it back as it opened. Esc closes the view.
- **The 3D model's file** is shown in the view, with its name and size. **Download** saves it, and every role can use it. **Replace** and **Remove** need permission to manage devices. Both ask first, so download the file before them if you have no copy. **Replace** deletes the file it replaces, and a link to that file stops working. After **Remove**, the device keeps its other details, and the file is deleted. Removing a model closes the view.
- **Add 3D Model…** takes the place of **View 3D Model** on a device with no model. It opens the same view with a drop zone. Drop a file on it, or click to choose one. It needs permission to manage devices. For anyone else it is greyed out, and its hover text names the roles that may. An archived device cannot take a new model, but one it has can still be viewed.
- **Who can open a model.** Anyone with a model's link can open it, because exported shells point at it. The drop zone says so. With a model attached, hover or focus **Public** in the view.
- **A replay lane has no 3D model of its own.** Its **View 3D Model** opens the model of the device it was recorded from. The view names that device beside the file's name. It is read-only. It has **Download** and **Reset view**, and no **Replace**, **Remove** or **Public**. A lane is never offered **Add 3D Model…**. If the device has no model, the lane's panel has no 3D model action at all. To add, replace or remove the model, open the device. The lane follows at once.
- **Suppression windows**, at the foot of the details panel, keep the device's alerts quiet during planned work. See [Suppression windows](#suppression-windows), below.
- **Export…** opens a dialog to choose the format. **AAS JSON (V3)** is the Asset Administration Shell as JSON. **AASX package** is the same shell packaged with any attached 3D model. **Bundle (with history)** is the AASX plus the device's Audit Trail, the readings still in the live database, and a manifest; a copy is kept beside the cold archive. The first two are open to every role. The bundle is disabled, with the reason, for a role that may not read the Audit Trail (the bundle contains it), and on a replay lane, which is a recording of a device rather than a device. The Entities page's Export Bundle opens the same dialog.
- **A complete Digital Nameplate names its template in an export.** The dialog marks each field the IDTA template asks for with an asterisk. Above its buttons it lists the ones still empty. Once they all have a value, the exported nameplate says which template it follows, and a viewer that knows the template shows it as one. Nothing has to be complete to be saved. Your device's own name is never used as the manufacturer's designation.
- **An exported shell names the device by more than its own id.** It also lists the device's serial number, its manufacturer's product URI and its Sparkplug id. Another system can then find the shell from any of them. The serial number and the URI are the ones in the shell's Digital Nameplate. A value that is empty is left out. A replay lane lists none, because it is a recording of a device.
- **The Quarantine tab** holds two kinds of device. One is a device Aber does not know: a gateway announced it, and nobody has registered it. The other is a registered device that is held again, because it came through another gateway or published a faulty ID. Either way its readings are dropped until it is approved, rather than stored against a guess.
- **Approve & Onboard** admits the device. For a device Aber does not know, the dialog starts from **Follow Gateway**. For a registered device it starts from the location and the gateway that device has, so approving it moves nothing. If the device came through another gateway, a line under **Serving Gateway** says which. Choose that gateway only if the device has moved there. With any other, it is held again at its next birth.
- **If a new device is one already registered under another name**, the queue offers that match instead. A device that was registered before and is held again is offered no match. **Accept Match** asks first, naming the device it keeps and the one it deletes, and says what carries over. **Reject** asks you to type the device's name. It deletes the device and the readings held for it, and cannot be undone. If the device publishes again, it comes back to Quarantine. Nothing in the queue counts as plant history until it is approved. Both actions need permission; a disabled button names the roles that have it.

## What the states mean

- **Awaiting first birth**: the record exists, but the device has not published yet. Usually a device created before it was installed. It is marked as a warning once it is more than 24 hours old.
- **Publishing unmodelled metrics**: the device sends metric names its schema does not include. It shows as a warning badge, **Unmodelled** with a count, in the **Status** column and beside the state in the details panel. Point at the badge to see the metrics. The top of the panel names them too. This is worked out when you look, not stored, so editing a schema updates its devices at once rather than at their next birth, which may be weeks away.
- **Out of vocabulary**: a metric's last value is not one of the **Permitted Values** its entry on the Metrics page lists. It shows as a warning badge in the same two places. Point at the badge to see which value. The top of the panel says it too. It is worked out from the last value when you look, so it shows the state now, not what happened earlier. It works even when the device's schema is older than those values.
- Where the Audit Trail holds a schema rejection for the device, the **Out of vocabulary** badge is a link, for a role that may read the Audit Trail. It opens the Audit Trail on that device's schema rejections, which is where earlier faults are kept.
- **Quarantined**: held until somebody approves it on the Quarantine tab. It is a device Aber does not know, or a registered one that came through another gateway or published a faulty ID. Quarantine is about identity, not readings. The four reasons are an unknown device, a malformed identifier, an identity mismatch and a gateway mismatch. The reason is stored, not just logged, so it is still there tomorrow. Whether readings match the schema is **Schema Conformance**, above.
- **Archived**: out of service. It leaves the Active list, and the **Archived** filter option counts it. The Entities page's **Archived** tab lists archived items of every kind.

**Device UUID** is the device's own ID in Aber, and it never changes. The API, the Audit Trail and an automation's file name the device by it.

**Sparkplug ID** is the ID Aber issues to a device. It is made from the device's UUID and never changes, so renaming or moving a device breaks nothing that points at it. Readings, exports and alerts all use it. In an exported file of readings, it is the `asset_id` column. Some devices cannot be set to publish it, such as those behind an edge node you bring yourself. A device like that keeps its own ID. The details panel shows it as **Published ID**, under the **Sparkplug Device ID**. The **Sparkplug Topic Path** there is the ID the device publishes under, joined to its gateway's group and edge node.

## Suppression windows

A suppression window keeps a device's alerts quiet during planned work, such as a belt change. An alert that fires during the window is still recorded. It is not counted, it colours nothing, and nothing is sent about it. The window ends by itself.

The details panel lists the device's current and upcoming windows under **Suppression windows**. Each shows its reason, its start and end in your local time, and who scheduled it. Point at the name to see the person's email address. **NOW** marks a window that has started. A window on the device's cell covers the device too. It is listed with **From cell** and the cell's name, and is changed from the cell's panel.

To schedule a window:

1. Open the device's details panel, and select **Schedule Window…**.
2. Give a reason. Everyone who can see the alerts reads it.
3. Choose a start and an end, in your local time. A start already past is now. The end can be at most 7 days after the start, unless your site has changed that.
4. Select **Schedule Window**.

**Change…** changes a window's end or its reason, and its start if it has not started. **End Now** ends a window early, so its alerts count again. A window that has not started has **Cancel Window** instead. Both ask first. When a window ends while an alert is still firing, the alert counts again, as if it had just fired.

Shopfloor Managers and Administrators can schedule, change and end windows. Everyone else can see them. Each is recorded in the Audit Trail, in the device's lane.

**A window lasts at most 7 days** unless an Administrator changes the limit, so a forgotten one still ends soon. The dialog names the limit under **End**. The limit is on the **Settings** page, on the **Retention** tab: **Longest suppression window (days)**, from 1 to 90. A change to a window's start or end is held to the limit too, counted from the window's own start. Lowering the limit leaves windows already scheduled as they are. Each still ends by itself.

The alert at the top of the details panel says whether it is firing, acknowledged or suppressed, and since when. An acknowledged one says who acknowledged it and why. Point at the name to see the person's email address. A suppressed one says until when, and the window's reason.
